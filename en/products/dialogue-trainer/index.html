Training loop

Dialogue Trainer is not a chatbot and not a script library. It is a training ground: the player speaks, the world responds, and the player returns to the next sentence carrying the review.

Enter a scene

Service, workplace, parenting, real-estate, and the long-running Ah-Fa restaurant world all open with concrete pressure, roles, and known facts.

Write the reply

Players respond in their own language. Beginners can turn on guardrails, but the core remains free-form input and real voice.

Watch consequence

AI makes the NPC continue to react, turning reputation, fatigue, patience, trust, and world memory into readable consequence.

Review the road

The journey journal keeps review notes, path labels, field imprints, and seven-day lookbacks so practice is never a one-time quiz.

What players practice

The entry is everyday life; the real training is language that can hold responsibility, boundaries, and the capacity for repair under pressure.

Ah-Fa Restaurant Seven-day language chessboard, reputation, fatigue, customer patience, and a fact ledger.
Din Tai Fung Service Responsibility, process closure, and customer reassurance in a high-standard service environment.
Company Manager Feedback, conflict, delegation boundaries, and executable commitments.
Parenting & Family Relationship repair, boundaries, holding emotion without abandoning responsibility.
Real-Estate Negotiation Information transparency, trust-building, and fair framing in high-value decisions.

Why it belongs here

Dialogue Trainer is the first product grown from the Three Realms Protocol academy layer. Players do not need to understand the protocol first — they can live through what the protocol describes.

Protocol into practice

Language rewrites the situation

A player says one sentence and the scene is rewritten. This is not an abstract concept — it is an interactive consequence visible every turn.

Academy into product

Live through it first, then understand

The academy's task is not to place deep concepts at the door but to design a path people can enter, practice on, and look back from.
